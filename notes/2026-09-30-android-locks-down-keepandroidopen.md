## Long summary

As of September 29 the campaign site keepandroidopen.org showed a countdown of 94 days until lockdown. Behind it are 71 organizations from 23 countries pressing Google over planned Android sideloading restrictions.

Today anyone can write an Android app and install it on any Android phone without permission or forms—the freedom called “sideloading,” which the piece says is the single reason stores like F-Droid can exist. Google’s plan, announced in August 2025: from 2027, every Android developer must register with Google (fee, government ID, app-signing keys, and a declared list of everything shipped). Skip registration and the app is silently blocked on every certified Android device worldwide—in practice nearly every phone from a major maker. The first enforcement wave starts September 30, 2026, in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, and Thailand; the worldwide rule follows in 2027.

Google’s escape route for holdouts is nine steps deep in Developer Options, has a 24-hour cooldown, and runs through Google Play Services—so Google can close it without an OS update. Useful for one curious person; a wall for a store shipping thousands of apps a day.

Signers of the campaign letter include GNOME, KDE, Brave, Proton, Nextcloud, the Tor Project, VideoLAN (VLC), Rocky Linux, Codeberg, the Software Freedom Conservancy, and the Chaos Computer Club. A change.org petition has passed 280,000 signatures. EFF has called app gatekeeping a pathway to censorship; Ars Technica (March 2026) said Google’s “Apple envy threatens to dismantle Android’s open legacy”; Cory Doctorow nicknamed the saga “Darth Android.”

In parallel, on September 24 F-Droid released version 2.0—its biggest update in ten years—rebuilt with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose (tabs: Discover, Search, My Apps; smarter search for Chinese, Japanese, and Korean). A new unified installer uses the pre-approval system the EU’s Digital Markets Act (DMA) forced onto big platforms, so installs from F-Droid on certified phones feel almost like the Play Store. Rollout begins over the coming weeks after 14 test releases; NLnet and the Open Technology Fund funded the work; the release passed a security audit.

For app publishers outside the Play Store, the piece points to FreeDroidWarn, a small open-source library by woheller69 that warns users about the coming changes. Non-developers can reach the letter and petition from keepandroidopen.org (countdown banner and “Post on X” button).

A closing detour: the team behind AIHOT open-sourced the system that runs the AI-edited news site; by Tuesday evening the repo had 2,861 GitHub stars (no Hacker News thread found); creators say about a million monthly readers (their figure, unverified). The piece closes that open source is building alternatives first—a redesigned store, a warning library, and a signed letter with 71 logos—before the countdown hits zero.
